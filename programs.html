The programs, in detail

Four programs of 8 to 12 weeks. Each one is mostly speaking, with corrections that target being understood.

Program 01 · Speak without rehearsing

Everyday English

Many adults can handle written English and freeze in a live conversation. We practice the situations that actually come up — a doctor's receptionist, a parent-teacher meeting, a neighbor on the stairs — and work on asking someone to repeat themselves without embarrassment.

  • 12 weeks
  • A2 to B1
  • 20 min a day
  • Headphones

Start this program

Weeks 1–3 · Getting started

Openings, closings and buying time while you think.

Weeks 4–6 · Real situations

Appointments, schools, services, shops.

Weeks 7–9 · Listening

Fast speech, accents and asking people to slow down.

Weeks 10–12 · Longer talk

Telling a story, giving an opinion, disagreeing politely.

Weeks 1–3 · Your field

The two hundred words your job needs, drilled.

Weeks 4–6 · Speaking up

Interrupting, clarifying and holding the floor.

Weeks 7–9 · Updates

Two-minute summaries that land.

Weeks 10–12 · Difficult moments

Disagreeing, negotiating and delivering bad news.

Program 02 · Be heard in meetings

English at work

Careers stall when capable people stay quiet in meetings. We rehearse interrupting politely, giving a two-minute update, asking a question that makes you sound competent, and pushing back on an idea — all in your own field's vocabulary.

  • 12 weeks
  • B1 and up
  • 3 hours a week
  • Your work context

Start this program

Program 03 · Clarity, not accent removal

Being understood

The goal is being understood the first time, not sounding American. We identify the handful of sounds and stress patterns that cause people to ask “sorry?”, and drill those. Your accent is part of you; nobody here will try to erase it.

  • 10 weeks
  • Any level
  • 15 min a day
  • Voice recordings

Start this program

Weeks 1–3 · Your sounds

The few that actually cause misunderstanding for you.

Weeks 4–6 · Word stress

Which syllable, and why it changes comprehension.

Weeks 7–8 · Rhythm

Sentence stress and the music of English.

Weeks 9–10 · Under pressure

Staying clear when you're nervous or speaking fast.

Weeks 1–2 · Structure

Subject, request, context — in that order.

Weeks 3–4 · Tone

Polite without being stiff, direct without being rude.

Weeks 5–6 · Hard emails

Chasing, declining, correcting a mistake.

Weeks 7–8 · Your phrasebank

Reusable phrasings for what you write every week.

Program 04 · Emails that sound natural

Writing at work

Written English at work is mostly about tone. Too formal reads as distant, too direct reads as rude, and textbook phrases sound odd. We work on your real emails, building a set of phrasings you can reuse for requests, follow-ups and declines.

  • 8 weeks
  • B1 and up
  • 2 hours a week
  • Your real emails
